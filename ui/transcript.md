## turn 1 (day 1)

> new game

You are Garrick: human, thirty-one, a magic user. You throw fire, and you can steal one moment out of a fight when you need it. The katana on your hip is masterwork and paid for. The purse holds a hundred and seventy-three gold. Years ago you signed with Hell -- wealth and power promised, obedience owed. Nothing has been asked of you in a while.

Meriele walks beside you: elf, thirty-two, bald, a zweihander across her back. She speaks carefully and she fights carefully, and she has watched your back for years.

You are in Castavera, capital of Mortellaria. White stone above the Stura River road, tiled roofs, the sea wind coming up the valley since dawn. There is a watch posted on the graveyard gate that was not there last season. The necromancers work openly in this country now and nobody on the market street wants to talk about it.

Maud the sheriff catches you at the foot of the palace steps. Bandits have taken three parties of travelers on the king's road inside a week. She knows roughly where the camp sits. She wants them killed and she wants it done inside two days.

```
  job: Bandits on the Road -- L6
  giver: Maud, the sheriff
  1 site, 1 fight. pays 92g, 308 XP
  due day 3 -- turned in now, x1.15
```

A bell starts at the river gate while she is still talking. A guard has come in off the northern border: orc raiders burning guard posts and mapping the roads. An orc warlord has united the clans and calls it the Sky's mandate. King Walt will be calling for blades before the day is out.

```
  *** WORD OF THE WAR ***
  [w1] L2 Orc Scouts -- 2 sites
  giver: Walt, king
  pays 79g, 290 XP
```

## turn 2 (day 3)

> i travel to ensimaa and check quests there

Two days on the road and nothing on it. Ensimaa arrives as trees before it arrives as a city: Taivelle stands in a stand of great living trees beside the Avelune River, timber halls and stone walks around an open market. You come in through the western gate with the rain going sideways. The storm has had the roads since yesterday. Half the market is packed away, the awnings are roped down, and the river is loud under the bridge.

"Observe the drainage," Meriele says, water running off her scalp. "They built the walks to shed it. My people think in centuries and still cannot be persuaded to roof a market."

There is no king here. The high council takes petitions at the Crown Hall, and the porch out of the wet is where the day's work gets handed out.

Uthemar, the circle elder, is young for the title and wants his problem settled quietly. A group of mages stole songs out of the circle that they had no right to, and they are still singing them. The mist standing over the western valley is theirs. He wants the ritual stopped and the singers dealt with.

Erevan, the council's wizard, has the worse problem and says so straight. One of their own has rebelled, taken a tower on the burned road, and is setting fires from it. He wants the man killed. He is paying three hundred and forty-six gold because nobody in Taivelle will go.

The rest of the porch is out of your weight entirely: a tribute owed to a dragon, and two warden jobs the judges have posted, one of them paying in steel.
