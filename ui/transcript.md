## turn 1 (day 1)

> please start a new game

You are Nerva, thirty-two, a Byzantine, and you have been taking paid work for years. You throw ice. The staff you carry is the best thing you own and it cost more than everything else in your kit together. Your purse holds 115 silver. Years ago you signed a contract with Hell -- wealth and power promised, obedience owed. Hell has been collecting the obedience ever since.

Varro walks beside you. He is seventeen, he looks older than that, and he has worked at your side since he was a boy. He carries a zweihander taller than he is, keeps his coat brushed, and stops for every dog in every village you pass through.

You are in Salicetum, a fishing village on the Byzantine coast, where the road comes down off the hills to a sheltered cove. Boats are drawn up on the shingle. The hills behind the village are terraced in vines, and the vines failed last year in the drought. Venice is an hour up the same road; the spice road out of Cairo ends at its docks.

Aurelia, the village priest, meets you at the graveyard wall. She is thirty-four and she has not slept. The dead walk out of this graveyard at night, she says. Two nights running, and last night one of them got as far as the fish sheds. The crypt under the yard is the village ossuary, the walls stacked with the village's own bones going back four hundred years, and she says the bones are not stacked any more. She wants you to go down and destroy what is in there. She can pay 166 silver and she wants it done inside the week.

```
  job: The Restless Crypt -- L5
  giver: Aurelia, village priest
  1 site, 2 fights. pays 166s.
  due day 7 -- 6 days left
```

A rider came up the coast road yesterday with the emperor's call-up. Umaia's army is over the border and Byzantium is taking every man in the east. The fighting is a long way from this cove.

There is a job board on the fish shed wall this morning. The wall was bare yesterday, and nobody in the village can see it. The work on it is yours: an altar in a chapel, blood on it before dawn, and the board says any blood will do, including your own. A fixer named Paulina holds the job here, and she lives in the village.

## turn 2 (day 1)

> doing the crypt job

Aurelia takes you up the coast road to Venice. Salicetum buries its dead outside the city wall, along with the poor of Petrapolis and Castranova -- one walled field for all three, and a hooded brotherhood to do the digging. It is an hour's walk uphill and the market noise comes over the wall most of the way.

The graveyard is flat stones in rough grass, with the city stacked up the slope behind it. A stone stair goes down into the ossuary at the far end of the field. Two stones near the wall have been pushed off and left lying. Four of the dead are standing among the graves, and they do not move when you come through the gate.

## turn 3 (day 2)

> camp, attack tomorrow

You sleep against the graveyard wall and nothing comes. In the morning Varro eats, checks the edge of his blade, and goes down the stair first after all.

Your first bolt takes the skeleton apart on the steps. After that it is Varro's fight: he kills all three ghouls himself, one a round, while your ice does little more than slow them down. One claw gets through and barely marks him. You finish it Winded with nothing left in the Power.

Nothing else moves down here, and nothing down here says what raised them. The graveyard above is quiet for the first time in three nights. Varro pushes the loose bones back onto the shelves with his boot, then with his hands, and does not say anything about it.

Aurelia is still at the gate. She does not go down to look. The work is done and the money is not: she settles up at Salicetum, an hour back down the coast road, and it pays 191 silver if you bring it to her while the job is still fresh.

Aurelia pays you in the churchyard at Salicetum, out of a box she keeps under the altar, and she counts it twice. Four of the dead were people she buried herself. She does not ask which. You get another nineteen silver out of her by pointing out that the job said one room and the crypt had two, and she pays that twice as well.

She walks back up the road that afternoon to bless the graves again. By evening the hooded men are at work in the ossuary putting four hundred years back on the shelves in the right order, and the village is talking about something else.

There are two more jobs in Salicetum and both of them are out of the village. Quintilla runs the sheep in the high pasture above the cove; a pack has been killing them and last week it killed one of her shepherds. She wants the wolves dead and the pelts brought down, and she will not pay on your word alone. Tullia is the sheriff here, twenty-two years old, and there is a bandit camp on the king's road she has nobody to send against. Travellers are being taken on that stretch. ## turn 5 (day 6)

> rest in the tavern then do the wolves

Beds at the tavern, two silver for the pair, and Varro eats like the meal is part of the wage.

Quintilla's flock is not above the village. It goes up the coast in summer, three days north, into the hills where the grass holds -- which is why she is standing in Salicetum with a dead shepherd and nothing she can do about it. She wants the pelts brought back down. Her word for it is that she has been told things before.

You go by water: out of the cove, then two days north along the coast with the rain coming steady on the second. A trader on the same boat says Umaian riders have burned a stretch of country in the east. Nobody aboard has been there.

You land on the sixth day in haze. The hill country runs up from the shore in dry terraces and old pasture, with a village called Lauretum inland and a hamlet above it. The high pasture is stone walls and thin grass a long way above the sea. Sheep are scattered across it, fewer than the walls were built for. The ground at the top wall is soft and covered in tracks, and three wolves are standing in them.
